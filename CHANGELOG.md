## [0.90.1] - 2026-09-23

- **Fixed: the middlewares and decorators answered `402` over a payment that may have moved.** A `402` tells the buyer to sign a new payment. The integrations (FastAPI `FastAPIX402`, `X402Depends`, `fastapi_require_payment`, `X402Middleware`; Flask `FlaskX402`, `flask_require_payment`; Django `DjangoX402Middleware`, `django_require_payment`, `X402PaymentView`; Lambda `LambdaX402.process_or_require`, `lambda_handler`; `require_payment`) sent one, or `400` in `require_payment`, for every failure that was neither a receipt-rail conflict nor transient. That included `502 settlement_unconfirmed`, where the facilitator broadcast the transaction and got no receipt, so it may be mined. They now answer:
  - **`500`** for a failure that names a transaction and is not transient (`502 settlement_unconfirmed`, any other `5xx` with a hash) and for any other `5xx` with `retryable: false`. The body adds `transaction` and `paymentId` at the top level when the facilitator sent them, its `reason`, `retryable: false`, `safeToReplay: false`, and a `message` telling the buyer not to sign another payment and to check the transaction first. No `Retry-After`. This is the TypeScript SDK's answer to `settlement_unconfirmed`.
  - **`409`** for an authorization the facilitator says was already used (`is_spent_nonce_error`), with `spentNonceEvidence` (`"structured"` or `"wording"`), the code as `reason` when a code said so, and `retryable: false`. This includes one answer that was `503`: a transient failure that also reads as spent, such as `503 idempotency_cache_corrupt`. A resend of it in a new handling carries a new key.
  - The receipt, when the facilitator sent one, travels in `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` on both.
- Unchanged: the order starts with the receipt rail's conflicts (`409`, or `503` in flight), and a `202 settlement_in_progress` stays `503` + `Retry-After` even when it names a transaction. `is_transient_error()`, its anti-double-settle guard and the settle retry policy are unchanged: the SDK re-sends none of these. A rejection keeps its answer: an invalid signature or insufficient funds is still `402` (`400` in `require_payment`). So is the opaque `400 contract_call_failed (ref: …)`: on EVM the facilitator reports a used authorization and an invalid signature the same way.
- Tests: `tests/test_integrations_undelivered.py` runs every middleware and decorator against a facilitator over a real socket for each case, plus mutations: without each new branch, its case gets the old answer.
- Docs: the README's seller section and "check in this order" example follow the integrations' order.

## [0.89.0] - 2026-09-23

Changes what a seller sends and answers. Read before upgrading.

- **Changed: one purchase binding per payment handling, on by default.** `X402Config.send_idempotency_key` defaults to `True` again. A handling of a payment (a `process_payment()` call, or a `settle_payment()` call with its retries and timeout fallback) sends ONE `Idempotency-Key` on `/verify`, `/settle` and the fallback's resend. The key is random (`new_idempotency_key()`, `x402-<64 hex>`, the shape the TypeScript SDK uses) unless the call passes `idempotency_key` (new, on all four methods) or `idempotency_scope`. The SDK never sends a key derived from the `X-PAYMENT` alone: whoever holds the payment can recompute such a key, and a facilitator with durable receipts (x402-rs 2.39.0) refuses to treat possession of the payment as a binding. The facilitator gives an admitted payment's answer back only to the binding that admitted it, so a seller whose settle timed out now recovers its own answer through the fallback.
- **Changed: `/verify` carries the same key as `/settle`.** `derive_idempotency_key(payload, op, scope)` returns the settle key for both operations: `x402-settle-…`, the value earlier releases already sent on `/settle`, so a purchase settled before the upgrade keeps its key. 0.83.0 to 0.88.0 sent `x402-verify-…` on `/verify`. The receipt rail does not bind that key, so the verify of a retried purchase answered `authorization_already_settled`. A derived key is a binding only while its scope is a secret of the seller.
- **Added: a replay the handling did not admit is refused.** A handling with no binding of its own (a fresh key, no `X-UVD-Purchase`) can receive `Idempotent-Replayed: true` before any of its own attempts could have admitted the payment. It then raises `PaymentSettlementError` with reason `authorization_already_settled` (`authorization_in_flight` for a `202`), carrying the receipt. Facilitators before 2.39.0 did not tie the replay to the binding; the SDK refuses it either way. A replay answering the handling's own timed-out or failed attempt is returned.
- **Added: the receipt rail's refusals, classified.** `admitted_authorization_code(exc)` names `authorization_already_settled`, `authorization_in_flight` and `receipt_request_conflict`, read from a `/settle` 409 or a `/verify` `invalidReason`; they are also exported as constants and `ADMITTED_AUTHORIZATION_CODES`. `payment_conflict_response(exc)` returns `(status, body, headers)`: `409` for settled and conflicting authorizations, `503` + `Retry-After` while in flight (the mapping the TypeScript SDK uses), and the receipt in `PAYMENT-RESPONSE`. Settled and conflicting authorizations read as spent (`is_spent_nonce_error`) and final. An authorization in flight is transient (`is_transient_error`), and `FacilitatorError.retryable` is true for it. A `202 settlement_in_progress` is transient, and the `retry=True` policy retries it under the same key.
- **Changed: the timeout fallback raises the facilitator's refusal.** A resend answered with one of those `409`s raises that `FacilitatorError` instead of a `TimeoutError`: resending the same request without the binding never gets the success back, and a timeout invited exactly that. With the key off, that 409 is probably the seller's own payment, but nothing proves it. Reconcile `receipt.settlement` before delivering.
- **Changed: the timeout fallback waits for a settle still in flight.** When the resend under the handling's own binding gets `202 settlement_in_progress` (the timed-out settle was admitted and is moving), the fallback asks again (same request, same key) for up to `SETTLE_IN_FLIGHT_POLL_SECONDS` (30), pausing as the facilitator says (`Retry-After` or the receipt's `retry.afterSeconds`, at most 5 s). The same request ends in its settle, delivered once. Past the budget it raises that `202` (transient, with the pending receipt) instead of a `TimeoutError`.
- **Added: `idempotent_replayed`** on `SettleResponse` and `PaymentResult`, read from the `Idempotent-Replayed` header and never from the body. **Added: `idempotency_key`** on `VerifyResponse`, `SettleResponse` and `PaymentResult`, excluded from `model_dump()` so it never reaches the buyer in `PAYMENT-RESPONSE`.
- **Changed: middlewares and decorators** (FastAPI `FastAPIX402`, `X402Depends`, `fastapi_require_payment`, `X402Middleware`; Flask `FlaskX402`, `flask_require_payment`; Django `DjangoX402Middleware`, `django_require_payment`, `X402PaymentView`; Lambda `LambdaX402`, `lambda_handler`; `require_payment`) answer those refusals with `409`, or `503` + `Retry-After` while in flight, and never deliver on them. They also answer every failure without a verdict with `503` + `Retry-After` and the facilitator's `reason` (`settlement_in_progress` for its `202`): a timeout, a settle still in flight past the fallback's budget, `503 idempotency_store_unavailable` / `receipt_store_unavailable`, another retryable `5xx`, a `429`. The buyer presents the same `X-PAYMENT` later and never signs another one. Before, Flask, Django, Lambda and `require_payment` answered those with `402` (or `400`), and FastAPI with `402` for a timeout or `503` without `Retry-After`. Rejections keep their previous answer.
- **Upgrading, networks without receipts:** their answers are unchanged, except for the key now being on. A keyed settle that the facilitator cannot check against its idempotency store gets `503 idempotency_store_unavailable` and does not settle, where a call without a key used to settle. That is no verdict: `is_transient_error` is true, and the SDK's integrations answer 503 + `Retry-After` so the buyer presents the same credential again. `send_idempotency_key=False` sends no key, as 0.88.0 did by default.
- **Removed:** the once-per-process warning for a keyed call without a scope; such a call now carries a fresh key. **Exported** `transient_503_response` from the package root; the README already imported it from there.
- Tests: doubles of three facilitators over real sockets (2.39.0 receipts, 2.36.0 to 2.38.0 receipts, no receipts, in `tests/receipt_rail.py`). They cover every middleware and decorator (including a settle that outlives its timeout and a facilitator store that cannot be read), plus mutation checks showing that without the guard a replayed settle is delivered. CI now installs the `fastapi`, `flask` and `django` extras, so the integration tests run there instead of being skipped. The 0.88.0 FastAPI receipt test failed in CI's resolution for lack of `fastapi`.
- Docs: EURC on Arc mainnet is confirmed with funded payments through the public facilitator (x402 v1 and v2, 2026-09-22, hashes in `docs/networks/arc.md`); Arc testnet funded acceptance stays pending. Remove three stale notes that still said EURC was absent from Arc (`CLAUDE.md`, the `networks` docstring, the README `get_networks_by_token` example output); `tests/test_readme_eurc_networks.py` pins that printed output to the registry. No runtime change.
